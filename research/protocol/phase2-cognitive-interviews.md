# Cognitive interview protocol — design, not a conducted study

**Purpose:** detect *comprehension, retrieval, judgment, and response mapping*
problems, not persuade respondents or test which ideology is correct.

## Eligibility / participant information

Define intended adult population, source language, geography, accessibility,
recruitment groups, incentives and retention first; obtain relevant review
and informed consent. Avoid recruitment via the anonymous production quiz.
Never store real participant transcripts or political answers in this repo.

## Semi-structured moderator flow

1. Explain that researchers are testing **questions**, not testing the
   participant's knowledge or political views. Declining is always allowed.
2. Present items in the allocated packet, one at a time. Record neutral
   think-aloud narration and actual answer selection only if consented.
3. For problematic items ask non-leading questions:
   - "What is this statement asking in your own words?"
   - "What does this term mean to you?"
   - "How did you choose your answer? Were two different claims combined?"
   - "Did you think about your city, your country, or another context?"
   - "Was there a response you wanted to give that was missing?"
4. Record whether the issue is comprehension, retrieval, judgment or
   response-option mapping. Keep direct quotes de-identified.
5. End with broad probes about **missing political domains**, overall burden,
   and an optional suggestion for improving a question.

## Coverage and iteration

Use initial 20 rotating packets per language as a *design candidate*;
each item is assigned twice in the proposed allocation. Actual interviews
may require revisiting high-risk items more often, further rounds, and new
probes after revisions. Evaluate themes across people and subgroups;
never treat two appearances as sufficient evidence of universal comprehension.
Perform translation/adaptation cycles separately for EN/PT.

Target a manageable duration and prioritize high-risk items if 24 items is
too burdensome. Plan recruitment with researchers, and maintain an audit
trail of item decisions after each round.
